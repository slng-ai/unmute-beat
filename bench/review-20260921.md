# Review of 2026-09-21: what it found, and what changed

`POC_BENCHMARK_REVIEW.md` reviewed commit `28516da` and reproduced defects in
verification, business completion, stale offers, idempotency, cancellation and
measurement. This is the revision-specific ledger it asked for. It does **not**
rewrite `baseline/`: those records are what was true when they were written,
and several of them overstate what is covered today. Read them together.

Everything below was closed offline, against the same scripted core the tests
use. Nothing here was measured on a live call, and none of it settles whether
this harness is faster than the native arm -- §5 of the review is a protocol
for two arms, and one of the two is not in this repository.

## Deliberately unchanged

* **The Spain-only phone policy.** The reference conversation uses
  `+14155550199` and `open_call` raises on it, so a literal replay of §5.2
  cannot start. Widening the validator would undo a fix made after a real
  call; normalising both arms onto one Spanish identity is a scope decision,
  not a bug fix. **This one needs a decision before the paired runs.**
* **`SMART_TURN_STOP_SECS`.** 1.5s, and the trace shows it costs exactly that
  on utterances the analyser calls incomplete. Lowering it needs a recording
  of what it cuts off.
* **The global `invalid-argument-type` suppression.** Measured with the rule
  on: 31 diagnostics, not the ~15 the note claimed, and five of them are
  inside the SDK where ty resolves an explicit `Agent[SalonCall, object]` as
  `Agent[object, str]` anyway. Making `Node` generic -- the documented upgrade
  path -- would not clear those five, so the line cannot come off with it.
  Both notes now say the measured number.
* **The shared-history / repeated-prompt workaround.** Documented in
  `voice/core.py` where it is done. The `messages`/`reset`/owner-restoration
  contract is a design change, not a defect fix, and nothing here claims
  portable typed conversation behaviour.

## Still open, and what it needs

* **Latency parity.** Unproven, in either direction. It needs the paired,
  interleaved runs of §5.3 against a frozen native arm, and the native arm's
  build is not identified by its trace.
* **The prompt-level changes of 28516da** -- the inlined documents, the "same
  time" rule -- and whether a real provider answers in prose once `speak_by`
  withdraws its tools. All three need one live call.
* **Interruption on disconnect, and between turns.** Before, during and after
  a tool are covered, and during fixed speech is covered; the other two are
  not.
* **`tests/fixtures/trace_native.json`** is the reference call, trimmed to the
  fields this reader reads. If the native arm is rebuilt, this fixture is of
  the old one.

## What the closed oracle then found

Running it is what the exactness is for. Two things the old check could not see:

* **The two arms have different consent policies.** This agency books on an
  unambiguous choice of an offered time -- `prompts/salon/booking.md` says to
  ask the confirming question *only* when the details are not already agreed
  -- and the native arm reads the booking back and waits for a separate yes.
  Both are defensible. They are not the same policy, and a policy that books
  a turn earlier finishes the call a turn sooner, which is a different
  product and not a faster one. It is recorded in `settings()` so a paired
  run states which policy each arm ran, and the booking turn count stays
  non-comparable until they are matched.
* **The complaint flow's write lands on a turn the model chooses.** The
  identity detour can come before or after the caller agrees to the summary,
  so that scenario pins the outcome and not the turn.

## Still open after this round

* Latency parity, in either direction. The paired protocol needs a frozen
  native arm, and the native build is not identified by its trace.
* The reference identity: `+14155550199` against a Spain-only validator.
  Normalising both arms onto one Spanish number is a decision, not a fix.
* Availability: three native offered times against this diary's capped
  half-hour grid, which is why the `reference` scenario pins no time.
* Audio: nothing here measures playout. Every timing in this repository is
  text handed to TTS.
* Interruption on disconnect and between turns.
* The shared-history workaround, and the `invalid-argument-type` suppression
  (31 diagnostics, five of them inside the SDK).

## What the traces of 12:14–12:24 then found

Three things, none of them in R1–R6, all reproduced off the Langfuse traces.

* **Release-blocking: the agency said a write had happened that never did.**
  Trace `ec513a99`, 12:23:22 — *"I've cancelled your appointment, and I have
  you down for a redo on Saturday at 2:30 PM."* The last write in that call
  was the move at 12:21:50; no `save_booking` runs after it, and the state
  still holds `action='move'` for Saturday 14:30. The following turn promised
  the redo would be free. Every guard in this repository constrains a write
  that happens; nothing constrains a sentence about one that did not.
* **`speak_by` counts a run's requests, not a turn's.** The same turn spent
  four requests — `hand_over`, `read_diary`, `hand_over` back, then the
  sentence above — and the cap never engaged, because `ctx.run_step` restarts
  at 1 for each agent. Across a hopping turn the request limit is still the
  only bound. Left alone for now: the ordinary flow (front desk, verify,
  booking, diary) is itself four requests, so a turn-wide cap of three would
  take the diary away from the main path.
* **The two arms count caller turns differently.** The POC's denominator is
  its `agents` spans and the native arm's is Pipecat's `turn` spans, and on a
  live call they are not the same number: `421a85c2` has 17 `agents` spans
  inside 14 Pipecat turns, because a caller who speaks twice before the bot
  answers runs the agency twice inside one turn. Requests per turn reads 1.41
  against the native arm's 2.29; on the one unit both arms have it is 1.85
  against 2.29. Not changed here — which unit is *the* caller turn is a
  decision about the benchmark, not a defect in it — but the headline
  overstates the gap by about a third until it is taken.

The narration guard is a correction, not a prevention: nothing can stop a
token that is already at TTS. What it buys is that the caller hears the

cannot see it will reward it.
